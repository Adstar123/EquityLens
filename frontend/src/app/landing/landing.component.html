Methodology

How a score is built

Every ticker page shows this working in full. Nothing is scored that can't be traced back to a filing, which also means a score moves when the company reports, not when the price does. Prices and valuation context refresh daily, while the fundamentals hold still between reporting seasons.

  1. 01

    Read the accounts

    Latest filings · BHP Group
    Revenue$58.8B
    Net profit$9.8B
    Total debt$28.4B
    Shareholder equity$56.3B

    Seven ratios are computed from each company's published financials, covering margins, returns, liquidity and leverage.

  2. 02

    Calibrate to the sector

    Debt to equity · Mining & Resources
    0.50
    PoorWeakNeutralGoodStrong

    Each ratio lands in a band set per sector. A debt load that's routine for a utility would raise flags at a miner.

  3. 03

    Weigh and score

    Composite · BHP Group
    78
    Asset turnover 8 / 20
    Debt to equity 14 / 17
    Return on equity 15 / 15

    Bands are weighted by what matters most in that sector, then rolled into a composite from 0 to 100.

Coverage

Eleven sectors, no single yardstick

Banks aren't miners. Six scoring models cover the eleven sectors. Sectors that run on the same economics share a model, and the rest get their own thresholds and weights. A score always means "strong for its kind".

What EquityLens won't tell you

Where the price goes next

Scores read filings, not sentiment. Momentum is somebody else's game.

What you should buy

A high score means strong fundamentals for its sector. It is never a recommendation.

More than the data can carry

When a filing is missing a figure, we score without it and say so on the ticker page.
